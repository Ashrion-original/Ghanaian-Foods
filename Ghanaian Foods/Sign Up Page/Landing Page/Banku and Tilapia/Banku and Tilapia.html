Ghanaian Foods

Origin

Banku originates from Ghana, predominantly associated with the Ga-Adangbe and Ewe people along the coastal and southern regions. Pairing it with grilled tilapia became a beloved national staple, particularly fueled by the commercial tilapia farming around Lake Volta.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups fermented corn dough
  • 1 cup cassava dough
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Water
  • 1 whole fresh tilapia (cleaned and scored)
  • Ginger, garlic, and onion
  • Scotch bonnet peppers (habaneros)
  • Fresh tomatoes
  • Vegetable oil
  • Bouillon cube and salt (to taste)

Procedure

  1. Marinate the tilapia: Blend ginger, garlic, onion, Scotch bonnet pepper, salt, and oil. Rub the marinade evenly inside and outside the scored tilapia.
  2. Grill the fish: Place the tilapia on a charcoal grill (or bake at 200°C / 400°F) for 25–30 minutes, turning halfway until fully cooked and charred.
  3. Make the dough slurry: In a deep pot, mix the corn dough and cassava dough with warm water and salt using your hands to break up all lumps until smooth.
  4. Cook the banku: Place the pot over medium heat and stir continuously with a wooden spatula (banku ta). Press the mass against the pot sides to smooth out lumps as it thickens.
  5. Steam and smooth: Pour a splash of water around the edges, cover, and steam on low heat for 5 minutes. Stir vigorously one final time until smooth and stretchy.
  6. Form and serve: Scoop into balls and serve warm with the grilled tilapia, fresh sliced tomatoes, onions, and ground hot pepper sauce.
