Ghanaian Foods

Gobe

Origin

Gobe (also known as Yɔɔ ke gari or Red Red) originated as a popular street food in Ghana, heavily associated with the Ewe community and urban working-class culture. It evolved into a national staple due to its affordability, high energy content, and rich combination of stewed black-eyed peas, red palm oil, gari (processed cassava granules), and sweet fried ripe plantains.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups black-eyed peas (soaked overnight)
  • 1/2 cup red palm oil
  • 1 cup gari (cassava flakes)
  • 2 ripe plantains (sliced into rounds or diagonal pieces)
  • 1 large yellow onion (sliced)
  • 2 fresh tomatoes (blended or chopped)
  • 1-2 Scotch bonnet peppers (blended)
  • 1 thumb-sized piece of ginger (grated)
  • 2 cloves garlic (minced)
  • Vegetable oil (for frying plantains)
  • Salt and seasoning cubes (to taste)
  • Water

Procedure

  1. Boil the beans: Drain the soaked black-eyed peas, place them in a pot with fresh water, and simmer over medium heat for 45–60 minutes until soft and tender. Season with salt toward the end.
  2. Fry the plantains: Heat vegetable oil in a skillet over medium heat. Fry the ripe plantain slices for 3–5 minutes per side until golden brown and caramelized. Drain on paper towels.
  3. Prepare the palm oil stew base: In a separate pot, heat the red palm oil over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and fry until golden and fragrant.
  4. Build the flavor: Stir in the grated ginger, minced garlic, blended Scotch bonnet peppers, and tomatoes. Cook the sauce for 10–15 minutes until the oil separates from the tomato base.
  5. Combine beans and stew: Pour the cooked beans (along with a bit of their cooking liquid) into the palm oil stew. Stir thoroughly and simmer for 10 minutes so the beans absorb the palm oil flavor. Adjust salt and seasoning.
  6. Assemble and serve: Dish the hot stewed beans onto a plate, sprinkle a generous layer of dry gari over the top, and serve alongside the warm fried ripe plantains.
