Ghanaian Foods

Fufu with Palm Nut Soup

Origin

Fufu with Palm Nut Soup (Abe Nwan) is a staple dish originating from the Akan people of Ghana, West Africa. Traditionally, cassava and green plantains are boiled and pounded into a smooth, elastic dough using a wooden mortar and pestle. The soup is made from concentrated oil palm fruit pulp, combined with assorted meats, fish, and aromatic spices, representing one of the richest traditional meals in West African cuisine.

Ingredients

  • 2 medium cassava roots (peeled and chopped)
  • 4 green plantains (peeled and chopped)
  • 1 can (800g) palm fruit concentrate (or fresh palm nuts extracted with hot water)
  • 1 lb beef, goat meat, or smoked fish
  • 1 medium onion (chopped)
  • 2 fresh tomatoes
  • 2 Scotch bonnet peppers
  • 1 thumb-sized piece of fresh ginger
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • Salt and seasoning cubes (to taste)
  • Water

Procedure

  1. Steam the meat: Season the meat in a pot with chopped onion, blended ginger, garlic, salt, and seasoning. Simmer over medium heat for 10–15 minutes until tender.
  2. Prepare the soup base: Mix the palm fruit concentrate with 4–5 cups of warm water to dilute it, then strain the liquid into the pot containing the meat.
  3. Cook the soup: Add whole tomatoes, whole Scotch bonnet peppers, and whole onions directly into the boiling soup. Let it simmer uncovered for 20–25 minutes until red oil starts to float on top.
  4. Blend and thicken: Remove the cooked tomatoes, peppers, and onions, blend them into a smooth paste, and stir them back into the soup. Continue simmering until the soup thickens to your preferred consistency.
  5. Boil for fufu: Place the chopped cassava and green plantains into a separate pot, cover with water, and boil until soft (about 15–20 minutes). Drain the excess water.
  6. Pound the fufu: Pound the warm plantain and cassava together using a mortar and pestle (or a high-powered food processor) until completely smooth, resilient, and elastic.
