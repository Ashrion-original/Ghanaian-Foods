Jollof Rice with Chicken

Origin

Jollof rice originated in the Senegambia region of West Africa among the Wolof people, where it was historically known as thiéboudienne (or chebu jen). The dish spread across West Africa during the colonial period, evolving into regional staples with distinct variations.

Ingredients

  • Grain: Long-grain Jasmine or Basmati rice (or parboiled long-grain).
  • Pepper Base: Fresh tomatoes, red bell peppers (tatashe), Scotch bonnets (kpakpo shito/atarodo), onions, garlic, and ginger.
  • Tomato Paste: Concentrated tomato paste for deep color and richness.
  • Seasoning & Spices: Curry powder, dried thyme, bay leaves, chicken or beef stock cubes, and salt.
  • Liquid & Fat: Vegetable oil and rich meat/chicken stock.

Procedure

  1. Blend tomatoes, bell peppers, Scotch bonnets, onions, garlic, and ginger into a smooth puree.
  2. Heat oil in a pot, fry remaining onions until clear, then cook tomato paste for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the blended puree and simmer until oil separates to the top.
  4. Season with curry powder, thyme, stock cubes, salt, and bay leaves, then stir in hot stock.
  5. Rinse rice until water runs clear and stir into the bubbling stew base.
  6. Cover pot tightly with foil and a lid to trap steam, then cook on low heat for 25 minutes.
  7. Let rest off the heat for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork and serve.
